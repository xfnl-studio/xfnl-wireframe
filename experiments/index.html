Experiments and Lab Notes

A Lab for Staying Curious.

I tinker with local AI to stay current with frontier models and to learn by building. Some of it turns into production tools, like the Automated Post-Production Assistant (APPA). Most of it teaches me something, and a few things teach me loudly.

01 / SystemRuns on Local HardwareNo Per-Token Fees

Waves

In plain English: Waves is a local LLM inference stack I designed and built with AI-assisted development (vibe coding). Three machines, four lanes, specialized models, and no cloud dependency, with a searchable index over my own notes and files.

Why the name: Sound waves, brain waves, energy, motion, and carrying the whole team. When the models focus together, that's clarity.

How it fits: small collectors watch for changes, rules react instantly, a filter drops anything unchanged, and then the models focus together as Waves: they cross-check each other and one writes the summary.

What it taught me: treat AI like infrastructure, with owners, fallbacks, and cost limits.

Built with: a 27B reasoning model, a 9B vision model, MCP servers, and Meilisearch.

Technical Details

A 27B reasoning model on a 24 GB GPU, a 9B vision model on a 12 GB GPU, prefix KV-cache pinning for fast first responses, custom MCP servers, and Meilisearch for search, and nomic embed on the Dell OptiPlex for embeddings. Three machines, four lanes.

02 / AgentRuns on WavesSwappable Model Underneath

Clarity

In plain English: Clarity is the friendly, curious agent on top of Waves. She asks good questions before she gives answers, and she runs on whichever local model I've swapped in. Her shape is a small cousin of the studio mark: a little web that turns to look at whoever is talking. Move your cursor, or tap, and she'll find you.

Talk to her: I can talk to Clarity from my phone through Siri, and she can kick off automations, including Home Assistant ones. A voice front door on top of Waves.

What it taught me: good questions beat fast answers, in AI and in intake.

Technical Details

Reasoning on a 27B local model, speech with Kokoro, search with Meilisearch, served with FastAPI. First words arrive in about 180 ms on my hardware with a warm cache.

03 / Pipelinea Function of WavesRuns Across All Four Lanes

APPA

In plain English: APPA is software that turns raw multi-camera footage into editable timelines for Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve. I built it as producer of Ben Windle Shoots His Shot to replace roughly 15 to 20 hours a week of manual logging and rough cutting. Time saved now: measuring.

The Showrunner: before APPA assembles a rough cut, a Showrunner pass compares a new shoot with the pacing of my best-performing shorts: how fast the cuts come, how soon the hook lands, and which story beats carry the joke. It acts as a developmental editor before I open Premiere or Resolve.

What it taught me: the hours leak in logging and rough cuts, not in creativity.

Built with: WhisperX, PySceneDetect, OpenTimelineIO, and FCPXML.

The Agents That Flooded My To-Do List

What I Tried

What Broke
They created well over a hundred tickets, most of everything on the board, and a reminder loop nagged me for hours.
What I Do Now
Agents get their own queue. Humans own the board.

The Parallax Pile-Up

What I Tried
I added scroll effects on top of one giant navigation script instead of refactoring it.
What Broke
Several effects fought each other, the browser froze, headers duplicated inside frames, and I broke my own live layouts.
What I Do Now
Refactor first, run one engine, and set a performance budget before adding motion.

Regex Surgery on Live HTML

What I Tried
I edited my site's HTML with blind find-and-replace scripts.
What Broke

What I Do Now
Content lives in a CMS, and a scrub check runs before every commit.
