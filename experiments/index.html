Experiments and lab notes

A lab for staying curious.

I tinker with local AI to stay current with frontier models and to learn by building. Some of it turns into production tools. Most of it teaches me something, and a few things teach me loudly.

01 / SystemRuns on local hardwareNo per-token fees

Waves

In plain English: Waves is the AI system I run on my own hardware. Four compute lanes work together, a searchable index covers my own notes and files, and nothing needs a cloud model to keep running.

How it fits: small collectors watch for changes, rules react instantly, a filter drops anything unchanged, and a council of models cross-checks before one writes the summary.

Built with: a 27B reasoning model, a 9B vision model, MCP servers, and Meilisearch.

Technical details

A 27B reasoning model on a 24 GB GPU, a 9B vision model on a 12 GB GPU, prefix KV-cache pinning for fast first responses, custom MCP servers, and Meilisearch for search. Three machines, four lanes.

02 / AgentRuns on WavesSwappable model underneath

Clarity

In plain English: Clarity is the friendly, curious agent on top of Waves. She asks good questions before she gives answers, and she runs on whichever local model I have swapped in. Her shape is a small cousin of the studio mark: a little web that turns to look at whoever is talking. Move your cursor, or tap, and she'll find you.

Talk to her: I can talk to Clarity from my phone through Siri, and she can kick off automations, including Home Assistant ones. A voice front door on top of Waves.

Voice to action

Siri to Clarity to APPA, in one breath.

Technical details

Reasoning on a 27B local model, speech with Kokoro, search with Meilisearch, served with FastAPI. First words arrive in about 180 ms on my hardware with a warm cache. Your results will vary.

03 / PipelineA function of WavesRuns across all four lanes

APPA

In plain English: Software that turns raw multi-camera footage into editable timelines for Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve. I built it as producer of Ben Windle Shoots His Shot to replace roughly 15 to 20 hours a week of manual logging and rough cutting. Time saved now: measuring.

The Showrunner: before APPA assembles a rough cut, a Showrunner pass compares a new shoot with the pacing of my best-performing shorts: how fast the cuts come, how soon the hook lands, and which story beats carry the joke. It acts as a developmental editor before I open Premiere or Resolve.

Built with: WhisperX, PySceneDetect, OpenTimelineIO, and FCPXML.

04 / PipelineA vision model as a quality gateRuns on Waves

The photo curator

In plain English: A four-step pipeline that picks real photographs for my daily digest instead of generating plastic stock art. A reasoning model writes a visual brief, the system searches my own photo library with a cooldown so nothing repeats, a local vision model grades the candidates for composition, sharpness, and crop, and I keep a veto.

Why it matters: most teams are drowning in AI slop. Here a vision model works as a quality gate over real photography, rejecting screenshots, blank walls, and blurry frames before a person ever sees them.

Built with: a reasoning model, a vision model, and a self-hosted photo library.

05 / EvaluationThree judges, one gateRuns on Waves

The triage judge

In plain English: I turned my job search into a small evaluation harness. An incoming posting is scored by three independent judges: a skeptical recruiter, an optimistic builder, and a local reasoning model. A hard fit floor stops weak matches before they reach my board.

How it works: the floor is a gate, not an average, and the probability estimate is designed to be calibrated against real outcomes as they come in. It is the same pattern I would use for creative intake: independent reviewers, a gate, and a feedback loop.

Built with: three independent scoring lenses and a local reasoning model.

Lab notes

Credits burned, lessons learned

I break things on my own time so I can be careful on yours.

The agents that flooded my to-do list

What I tried
I wired automation and overnight scripts straight into my personal task board.
What broke
They created well over a hundred tickets, most of everything on the board, and a reminder loop nagged me for hours.
What I do now
Agents get their own queue. Humans own the board.

The parallax pile-up

What I tried
I added scroll effects on top of one giant navigation script instead of refactoring it.
What broke
Several effects fought each other, the browser froze, headers duplicated inside frames, and I broke my own live layouts.
What I do now
Refactor first, run one engine, and set a performance budget before adding motion.

Regex surgery on live HTML

What I tried
I edited my site's HTML with blind find-and-replace scripts.
What broke
Tags broke, text doubled, and CSS vanished.
What I do now
Content lives in a CMS, and a scrub check runs before every commit.

Image slop

What I tried
I let an image model and unranked photo queries pick the hero art for a daily digest.
What broke
I got generic AI slop, screenshots, and photos of doorframes.
What I do now
A four-step editorial hierarchy, a vision model that grades candidates, and a human veto.
